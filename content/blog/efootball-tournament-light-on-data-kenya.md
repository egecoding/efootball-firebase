"Is it an app?" is usually the first question when someone shares a tournament link in a Kenyan group chat, and it's a fair one — a lot of players are on limited bundles, and nobody wants to burn data downloading something just to join one tournament.

## It's not an app you install

There's nothing to download from a store and nothing that sits on your phone updating itself in the background. You open a link, same as you would to read any webpage, type your name, and you're in the bracket. When the tournament's over, there's nothing left behind to delete either.

## What actually costs you data

- **Opening the link once** to join — about what loading any lightweight webpage costs.
- **Checking the bracket or standings** when you want to — you only load data when you actually open the page, not constantly in the background.
- **Submitting a result with a screenshot** — a bit more, since you're uploading an image, but only when you actually report a match.

There's no ongoing sync, no push notifications draining data in the background unless you've chosen to enable them, and nothing running when the app isn't open in your browser.

## Why this matters more than it sounds like

A lot of organizing in Kenya happens on shared or limited data — a bundle that has to last the week, a phone that's not always on wifi. A tool that asks players to download something first is already asking too much before the tournament's even started. One that just works off a link, the same way opening a WhatsApp group does, doesn't add that cost.
