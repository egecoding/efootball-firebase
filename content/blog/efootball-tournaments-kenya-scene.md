Kenya's eFootball scene is more organized than it looks from the outside. There's a real competitive pathway — the Football Kenya Federation runs eFootball qualifiers (still called "FIFA qualifiers" by a lot of players out of habit — [more on that naming mix-up](/blog/fifa-vs-efootball-naming-explained-kenya)), and national events like the Kenya eFootball National Qualifiers have sent players through to the IESF world stage. It's a legitimate scene, not just people playing at home.

Most players never touch that pathway, though. What they actually do is play their own tournaments — with their squad, their block, or the regulars at their local lounge — organized in a WhatsApp group with a screenshot of a hand-drawn bracket. That works, until the group gets past eight people and someone loses track of who plays who next, or two people disagree about a scoreline and there's no way to settle it.

## You don't need an official qualifier to run a real tournament

The gap between "official FKF event" and "argument in a WhatsApp group" is exactly where a proper bracket tool fits. You don't need a federation, a venue booking, or a budget — you need an organizer, a group of players, and about two minutes to set it up.

Here's what that looks like:

1. **Pick a format.** A knockout is the fastest option for a one-night squad tournament — if you want something with less "one bad game and you're out," a [double elimination or round robin might fit better](/blog/choosing-the-right-tournament-format).
2. **[Create the tournament](/tournaments/new)** and get a shareable link.
3. **Drop the link in your group chat.** Everyone [joins by typing their name](/blog/no-account-needed-guest-players) — no downloads, no accounts for players, just the link straight into the bracket.
4. **Matches get tracked automatically**, so nobody has to remember or redraw anything, and a screenshot settles any scoreline dispute instead of an argument in the group chat.

## Why this matters more in Kenya than it might elsewhere

Most players here are gaming from a phone or a rented console at a lounge, not a personal setup at home — which is exactly why the whole thing runs from a browser link instead of an app you'd need to install. It's also why the invite-link-in-WhatsApp flow matters so much: WhatsApp is already how these tournaments get organized, so the tool just has to slot into that instead of asking anyone to change habits.

Whether you're running it for your estate, your [campus](/blog/efootball-tournament-campus-hostel-kenya), or your [lounge's regulars](/blog/efootball-tournament-gaming-lounge-kenya), the format is the same one behind the official scene — it's just yours, tonight, for free.
