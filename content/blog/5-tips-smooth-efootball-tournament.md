Anyone who's organized a few of these picks up the same lessons eventually. Save yourself the trouble and start with them.

## 1. Agree on the rules before anyone plays a match

Extra time on a draw? Which stadium, weather, difficulty? Are walkovers allowed for no-shows, and after how long? None of this needs to be complicated, but it needs to be decided **before** the first match, not argued about after someone loses. Post it once in your group chat or in the tournament description, and point back to it if anyone asks.

## 2. Match the format to your group size, not the other way around

A knockout with 6 players means three of them are out after one game — not exactly a fun Tuesday night for the guy who lost 1-0 in the 89th minute. If your group is small (4-8 people), a [league or round robin](/blog/choosing-the-right-tournament-format) gives everyone more games and a fairer overall result. Save knockouts for bigger groups where a fast bracket actually makes sense.

## 3. Ask for screenshots, every match, no exceptions

Make it a habit from tournament one, not just "when there's a dispute." If everyone knows a screenshot is expected every time, nobody feels singled out when you ask for one on a contested result — and the platform can [read the score straight off it](/blog/ai-screenshot-scanning-and-fair-results) automatically, so it costs players nothing extra.

## 4. Set a deadline for each round, not just the whole tournament

"Finish your matches by Sunday" without a per-round deadline is how tournaments die halfway through — two stragglers hold up everyone else, momentum drops, and half the bracket forgets it's still going. Give each round its own short window (48-72 hours works well for most groups) and use a walkover for no-shows past the deadline.

## 5. Share the link everywhere your group actually is, not just one place

Your invite link works the same whether you drop it in WhatsApp, Discord, or a group text — and because [joining needs no account](/blog/no-account-needed-guest-players), there's zero friction for anyone who clicks it. Post it in every channel your group actually uses instead of assuming everyone checks the same one.

None of this is complicated, but it's the difference between a tournament that finishes with everyone still talking to each other and one that fizzles out in round two. [Create your tournament](/tournaments/new) and put these into practice from match one.
